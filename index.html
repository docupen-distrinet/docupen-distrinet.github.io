DocuPen.

The Role of Design Documentation in Supporting Web Application Penetration Testing

Overview

What this study is about

DocuPen is a research project at KU Leuven studying how different forms of supporting documentation and resources influence the effectiveness and efficiency of web application penetration testing. Findings will help improve the documentation, training materials, and practices available to testers, developers, and students.

If you take part, you will carry out a penetration test on a purpose-built web application using our experimental platform, with documentation describing the application provided before you begin. Short surveys before and after the session collect your professional background and your impressions of the task. The platform records your findings, timestamps, and your interaction with the documentation — see "How your data is handled" below.

Who can take part

You can take part if you are 18 or over and have some hands-on experience with web application penetration testing. There is no minimum number of years or professional requirement: professionals, engineers, researchers, and students are all welcome, as long as you have done this kind of testing before.

Sessions are run remotely and individually. All materials and surveys are in English; basic English is enough for the session itself. The optional follow-up interview requires enough English to hold a conversation.

Compensation

Participants who complete the full study — the pre-experiment survey, the penetration testing session, and the post-experiment survey — receive an Amazon voucher of approximately 30 EUR.

Participants who also complete the optional follow-up interview receive an additional Amazon voucher of approximately 15 EUR.

Compensation terms

Compensation is not provided to people who register during recruitment but do not go on to take part in and complete the full study. If you withdraw before completing all parts of the study, you will not receive compensation.

Additional Information

What participation involves

One session of approximately 3 hours (remote, solo), with optional parts that can extend it by up to a further hour:

  1. Read the study introduction ~5 minutes

    Read the experiment procedure, which explains what you will be asked to do.

  2. Review and provide informed consent ~5 minutes

    Review the informed consent form and give your consent before anything else begins.

  3. Complete a short pre-experiment questionnaire ~5 minutes

    Covers your background, penetration testing experience, and familiarity with documentation.

  4. Review the concept of documentation used in this study ~10 minutes

    A short introduction to the type of documentation available during the experiment.

  5. Learn how to use the platform to conduct the study ~10 minutes

    A walkthrough of the platform — how to view the documents, reach the target application, and submit or update findings.

  6. Review experimental design documents and take a few quizzes ~15 minutes

    Review the documentation for your assigned application, then answer a short multiple-choice questionnaire so we can confirm the material was understood.

  7. Perform penetration testing on a web application and submit your findings ~120 minutes, optionally +30

    Carry out the penetration test on the assigned application, submitting findings through the platform as you go. You may choose to continue for around 30 minutes more.

  8. Complete a short post-experiment questionnaire ~15 minutes

    Covers the usefulness of the documentation, your workload during the task, and your testing strategy.

  9. Debriefing and thank you ~5 minutes

    Receive a debrief explaining the purpose of the study.

  10. Optional interview ~30 minutes

    The option to take part in a follow-up interview, arranged for a separate time slot after the experiment.

You don't need to do this in one straight 3-hour sitting.

  • Steps 1–5 can be completed at any time within 48 hours of receiving your access credentials.
  • Steps 6–9 unlock at your scheduled testing time. Only the penetration test itself (step 7, up to 120 minutes with the optional extension) needs to run as one continuous block — the rest doesn't have to be back-to-back with it.
  • Step 10 (the optional interview) is arranged for a separate time slot after the experiment — it does not need to happen right away.

The surveys ask about your professional background — years of web application penetration testing experience, familiarity with particular documents, certifications — and your impressions of the task. They do not ask sensitive or personal questions.

Risks and discomforts

The task involves moderate cognitive workload and time pressure. The risk of stress is low. All testing takes place in a controlled experimental environment, and no personally sensitive data is exposed.

How your data is handled

Your personal data is processed in line with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). Only data strictly necessary to achieve the research objectives is processed. Your data is kept confidential throughout the study, and we take measures to protect your privacy. Your personal data is pseudonymised, meaning it can no longer be linked to you without additional information accessible only to the researchers. Pseudonymised data may be reused for other scientific research, and possibly for teaching or academic lectures.

The penetration testing session is not audio or video recorded. The platform logs clicks, mouse hovers over documents, finding submissions, your interactions with the target application, and your survey answers. These logs are collected only within the study platform itself — they do not include activity in other browser tabs, other applications, or anything outside the experiment environment.

If you take part in the optional follow-up interview, that interview is recorded in audio and video. The recording is transcribed and pseudonymised, and the original recording is deleted once the transcript has been verified.

Voluntary participation

Taking part is entirely voluntary. You can stop participating at any time, without giving a reason, and without any negative consequences for you.

Why we don't tell you the research question up front

We do not disclose the exact research question before or during the study, because knowing it could change how you approach the task and bias the results. All tasks, procedures, and data collection are explained to you in full before you consent, and you are debriefed on the specific research question once the study concludes.
