DocuPen.

The Role of Design Documentation in Supporting Web Application Penetration Testing

Overview

What this study is about

DocuPen is a research project at KU Leuven studying how different forms of supporting documentation and resources influence the effectiveness and efficiency of web application penetration testing.

If you take part, you will carry out a penetration test on a purpose-built web application using our experimental platform. You will be given documentation describing the application before you begin. The platform records your findings, timestamps, and your interaction with the documentation provided. Short surveys before and after the session collect your professional background and your impressions of the task.

Our aim is to understand how documentation can be designed to better support penetration testing, and to improve the training materials and practices available to testers, developers, and students.

Who can take part

Testers, professionals, engineers, researchers, or students with experience in web application penetration testing.

Compensation

Participants who complete the full study — the pre-experiment survey, the penetration testing session, and the post-experiment survey — receive an Amazon voucher of approximately 30 EUR.

Participants who also complete the optional follow-up interview receive an additional Amazon voucher of approximately 15 EUR.

Compensation terms

Compensation is not provided to people who register during recruitment but do not go on to take part in and complete the full study. If you withdraw before completing all parts of the study, you will not receive compensation.

Additional Information

What participation involves

One session of approximately 3 hours, with optional parts that can extend it by up to a further hour:

Session stages and approximate time
Stage Time
Consent and pre-experiment survey~15 minutes
Introduction to the documentation and platform tutorial~15 minutes
Reviewing the documentation for your assigned application~10–15 minutes
Penetration testing task on one web application~120 minutes, optionally extended by ~30 minutes
Post-experiment survey and debrief~15 minutes
Optional follow-up interview~30 minutes

In more detail, you will:

  • Read the experiment procedure and the informed consent form, and give your consent before anything begins.
  • Complete a pre-experiment survey covering your background, penetration testing experience, and familiarity with documentation.
  • Receive a short introduction to the type of documentation available during the experiment, and a walkthrough of the platform — how to view the documents, reach the target application, and submit or update findings.
  • Review the documentation for your assigned application, then answer a short multiple-choice questionnaire so we can confirm the material was understood.
  • Carry out a 120-minute penetration test on the assigned application, submitting findings through the platform as you go. You may choose to continue for around 30 minutes more.
  • Complete a post-experiment survey on the usefulness of the documentation, your workload during the task, and your testing strategy.
  • Receive a debrief explaining the purpose of the study, and the option to volunteer for a follow-up interview.

The surveys ask about your professional background — years of web application penetration testing experience, familiarity with particular documents, certifications — and your impressions of the task. They do not ask sensitive or personal questions.

Risks and discomforts

The task involves moderate cognitive workload and time pressure. The risk of stress is low. All testing takes place in a controlled experimental environment, and no personally sensitive data is exposed.

How your data is handled

Your personal data is processed in line with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). Only data strictly necessary to achieve the research objectives is processed. Your data is kept confidential throughout the study, and we take measures to protect your privacy. Your personal data is pseudonymised, meaning it can no longer be linked to you without additional information accessible only to the researchers. Pseudonymised data may be reused for other scientific research, and possibly for teaching or academic lectures.

The penetration testing session is not audio or video recorded. The platform logs clicks, mouse hovers over documents, finding submissions, your interactions with the target application, and your survey answers. These logs are collected only within the study platform itself — they do not include activity in other browser tabs, other applications, or anything outside the experiment environment.

If you take part in the optional follow-up interview, that interview is recorded in audio and video. The recording is transcribed and pseudonymised, and the original recording is deleted once the transcript has been verified.

Voluntary participation

Taking part is entirely voluntary. You can stop participating at any time, without giving a reason, and without any negative consequences for you.

Why we don't tell you the research question up front

We do not disclose the exact research question before or during the study, because knowing it could change how you approach the task and bias the results. All tasks, procedures, and data collection are explained to you in full before you consent, and you are debriefed on the specific research question once the study concludes.
